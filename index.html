Australia's Tourism Accommodation

Exploring national performance metrics across accommodation classes and state jurisdictions.

Domestic Resilience and Global Reconnection

The pandemic fundamentally altered the balance of Australia's visitor economy, creating two distinct recovery paths. With international borders closed, the accommodation sector survived primarily because local travellers redirected their spending across regional and interstate destinations. This sustained domestic demand buffered hotels from complete operational collapse and established a solid baseline that continued expanding even after border restrictions were lifted.

In contrast, overseas visitor spend effectively vanished overnight. While international travel has since mounted an aggressive multi-year comeback across major transit corridors and commercial gateways, total market value remains heavily tilted toward Australian residents traveling within their own borders.

Accommodation Rebound Trajectory

Accommodation services emerged as the primary growth engine of Australia's internal tourism economy[cite: 14]. While industry-wide spending contracted severely during border closures in 2020–21, accommodation expenditure experienced an aggressive turnaround, expanding from $10.1B at the pandemic trough to $21.8B in 2024–25[cite: 4, 11, 14].

Market Breakdown

Hotels rank highest among accommodation types, driving the vast majority of industry revenue compared to serviced apartments, resorts, and motels[cite: 5, 7]. Across the broader tourism market, core accommodation makes up the largest share, closely followed by food and beverage spending[cite: 5, 7].

Key International Feeder Markets

International guest arrivals remain heavily reliant on proximate regional neighbours and traditional English-speaking corridors. New Zealand stands out as the largest single contributor to overseas visitation, while East Asian markets—principally China, Japan, and South Korea—collectively funnel substantial visitor traffic into major Australian gateways. Long-haul arrivals from the United States and the United Kingdom continue to provide steady demand, ensuring a diverse balance between regional short-haul visitors and high-value long-stay travellers.

Performance Tiers and Revenue Concentration

Occupancy rates track directly with property tier, with luxury and upscale establishments maintaining higher, more stable room utilisation across every season[cite: 6]. In contrast, mid-scale and budget options experience softer demand throughout the year, widening the performance gap between market segments[cite: 6].

Gross takings remain heavily concentrated along the eastern seaboard[cite: 6]. New South Wales commands the largest market volume by a clear margin, with Queensland and Victoria following, while the remaining states and territories generate smaller regional revenue footprints[cite: 6].

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), Survey of Tourist Accommodation (STA)[cite: 6].

Regional Pricing Power and Demand Disparities

Key metropolitan centres, particularly Sydney, Melbourne, and Perth—combine strong room rates with elevated occupancy levels, reflecting stable business activity and continuous leisure travel[cite: 7]. Meanwhile, leisure hubs such as the Gold Coast and Cairns maintain consistent seasonal pricing[cite: 7].

In contrast, northern regional markets such as Darwin experience pronounced seasonal divergence, characterized by sharp off-peak occupancy drops even as Southern and Western capital gateways sustain solid operational baselines[cite: 7].

Shift Toward Commercial Stays

While private stays with friends and family continue to absorb the largest share of total overseas visitor nights, international demand has shifted strongly toward commercial lodgings[cite: 7]. Hotels, resorts, and short-term rental homes experienced the strongest year-on-year growth, showing that returning overseas travellers are increasingly prioritising paid accommodations[cite: 7].

Conversely, budget and youth sectors like backpacker hostels lagged behind, contracting year-on-year and highlighting a distinct divergence in how different international traveler segments choose to stay[cite: 7].

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) / Tourism Research Australia (TRA), International Visitor Survey (2024)[cite: 7].

Accommodation Leads Sector Employment

Accommodation served as the primary employment driver across the entire tourism economy, adding more filled positions than every other growing sector combined[cite: 7]. The operational demand required to service returning domestic and international guests forced hotels to aggressively rebuild their front-line workforces[cite: 7].

Meanwhile, passenger transport and general tourism services saw net contractions, showing that industry labour recovery remains heavily centred on lodging rather than transit networks[cite: 7].

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), Tourism Satellite Account (TSA) Table 5 (Filled Jobs)[cite: 7].

Development Pressure and Supply Disparity

Capital additions reveal diverging supply conditions across key markets[cite: 8]. Melbourne added the largest concentration of new hotel rooms, putting downward pressure on utilisation even as broader state occupancy remains steady[cite: 8]. Sydney and Brisbane saw moderate additions, keeping supply in closer balance with incoming visitor volume[cite: 8].

Smaller capital markets like Adelaide, Perth, and Hobart saw limited room expansion, preserving tighter baseline occupancy across their accommodation networks[cite: 8].

Source: CoStar / The Australian Financial Review (AFR)[cite: 8].

Rising Costs Threaten Industry Sustainability

While visitor volumes and revenues have largely normalised across Australia, rising overheads continue to pressure operator profit margins[cite: 9]. Energy costs, taxation, and labour expenses are the most widespread operational burdens, exceeding typical industry averages[cite: 9].

Combined with increasing capital investment costs and economic uncertainty, long-term profitability increasingly depends on managing persistent cost inflation and operational expenses[cite: 9].

Source: Statista / Booking.com Accommodation Barometer[cite: 9].
